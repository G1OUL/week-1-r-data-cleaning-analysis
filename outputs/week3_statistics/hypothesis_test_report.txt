[RQ1] Is educational attainment associated with income category?
  Null Hypothesis:        H0: Education and income are independent.
  Alternative Hypothesis: H1: Education and income are statistically associated.
  Method:                 Pearson's Chi-Square Test of Independence (Sample N = 32,537)
  Descriptive Stats:      16 Education categories; N = 32,537 (<=50K: 24,698, >50K: 7,839)
  Test Statistic:         Chi-Square = 4428.4 (df = 15)
  Unadjusted P-Value:     < 0.0001 (Raw: 0.000000e+00)
  Bonferroni Adjusted p:  < 0.0001
  Benjamini-Hochberg FDR: < 0.0001
  Effect Size:            Cramer's V = 0.3689
  Assumption Diagnostic:  Min expected count = 12.0; 0% cells < 5. Cochran rule satisfied.
  Statistical Decision:   Reject H0 at alpha = 0.05
  Practical Conclusion:   Statistically significant association between education and income (chi2 = 4428.4, df = 15, p < 0.0001, V = 0.369; moderate effect size).

[RQ2a] Is average age significantly different between income groups?
  Null Hypothesis:        H0: Mean age <=50K equals mean age >50K.
  Alternative Hypothesis: H1: Mean age differs significantly between income groups.
  Method:                 Welch Two-Sample t-test (unequal variances) (Sample N = 32,537)
  Descriptive Stats:      <=50K: Mean = 36.79 (SD = 14.02) vs >50K: Mean = 44.25 (SD = 10.52)
  Test Statistic:         t-statistic = 50.24 (df = 17406.1)
  Unadjusted P-Value:     < 0.0001 (Raw: 0.000000e+00)
  Bonferroni Adjusted p:  < 0.0001
  Benjamini-Hochberg FDR: < 0.0001
  Effect Size:            Cohen's d = 0.5629
  Assumption Diagnostic:  Mild right-skewness (+0.56); robust by Central Limit Theorem (N = 32,537). Welch correction protects against heteroscedasticity.
  Statistical Decision:   Reject H0 at alpha = 0.05
  Practical Conclusion:   High earners are significantly older on average (mean 44.25 vs 36.79 yrs; t = 50.24, p < 0.0001, Cohen's d = 0.56; moderate-to-large effect).

[RQ2b] Does the age distribution median differ between income groups (non-parametric)?
  Null Hypothesis:        H0: Median age and distribution of age are identical across income groups.
  Alternative Hypothesis: H1: Age distribution is shifted towards older individuals in the >50K group.
  Method:                 Wilcoxon Rank-Sum Test (Mann-Whitney U) (Sample N = 32,537)
  Descriptive Stats:      <=50K: Median = 34 (IQR = 21) vs >50K: Median = 44 (IQR = 15)
  Test Statistic:         W-statistic = 132460134 (df = NA)
  Unadjusted P-Value:     < 0.0001 (Raw: 0.000000e+00)
  Bonferroni Adjusted p:  < 0.0001
  Benjamini-Hochberg FDR: < 0.0001
  Effect Size:            Rank-Biserial r = 0.3683
  Assumption Diagnostic:  Distributional shape similar between groups; ordinal ranking assumption met. Exact = FALSE due to tied ages.
  Statistical Decision:   Reject H0 at alpha = 0.05
  Practical Conclusion:   Median age of high earners is significantly greater (median 44 vs 34 yrs; W = 132460134, p < 0.0001; non-parametric effect r = 0.3683).

  Practical Conclusion:   High earners work significantly more hours per week on average (mean 45.47 vs 38.84 hrs; t = 45.10, p < 0.0001, Cohen's d = 0.55; moderate effect).

[RQ5] Do education years and weekly hours show a significant linear relationship?
  Null Hypothesis:        H0: True correlation between education_num and hours_per_week equals 0.
  Alternative Hypothesis: H1: True correlation is significantly different from 0.
  Method:                 Pearson's Product-Moment Correlation Test (Sample N = 32,537)
  Descriptive Stats:      Edu Num Mean = 10.1 (SD = 2.6); Hours Mean = 40.4 (SD = 12.3)
  Test Statistic:         t-statistic = 27.07 (df = 32535)
  Unadjusted P-Value:     < 0.0001 (Raw: 1.260232e-159)
  Bonferroni Adjusted p:  < 0.0001
  Benjamini-Hochberg FDR: < 0.0001
  Effect Size:            Pearson's r = 0.1484
  Assumption Diagnostic:  Bivariate linearity inspected; discrete education scale mildly violates strict normality but CLT ensures accurate inference.
  Statistical Decision:   Reject H0 at alpha = 0.05
  Practical Conclusion:   Statistically significant but weak positive correlation (r = +0.1484, t = 27.07, p < 0.0001, 95% CI [0.1378, 0.1590]).

  METHODOLOGICAL NOTES ON STATISTICAL VS. PRACTICAL SIGNIFICANCE
  Because N = 32,537 is large, standard errors are exceptionally small, causing

  penalties. Therefore, effect sizes (Cramer's V, Cohen's d, Pearson's r, and
  rank-biserial r) are critical to distinguishing statistically detectable
  relationships from practically meaningful economic and sociological disparities.
